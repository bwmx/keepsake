# Paper alignment plan

Drafted 2026-10-09; Phases 0–3 done the same day. This is a design proposal; no cryptographer has reviewed it yet, and none of it is implemented except where marked done. It aims to move the demo from one hidden committee per capsule to the paper's evolving-committee proactive secret sharing (ECPSS, [eprint 2020/464](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/464) §3) on Algorand. The stack stays the same: Puya TS contract, TypeScript off-chain. Section numbers (§) refer to the paper.

## Where we are

Already done and matching the paper:
- per-epoch nomination keys with erasure (§2.1, §3.1.3);
- private VRF sortition;
- nomination through sealed ephemeral keys and trial decryption (§3.1.2);
- honest-dealer initial sharing (§3.1.1).

Remaining gaps:

1. **No handover.** A capsule's committee keeps its shares for the capsule's whole life. Security therefore assumes fewer than t corrupt holders over that whole period, not per epoch.
2. **Fixed slots with one winner each.** The paper gives one seat to every sortition winner, ordered by ephemeral key (§3.1.2 step 5).
3. **Committee size and threshold.** We use 3–64 holders and t = ⌊n/2⌋+1. The paper derives both from the corrupt fraction f (§3.2).
4. **No verifiable re-sharing.** Shares are not encrypted in a way that can be proven correct (§2.5, §2.6).
5. **One committee per capsule.** The paper runs one committee per epoch for the whole system (Fig. 1).
6. **No stake weighting and no §4 recovery model.**
7. **Randomness from a single-operator beacon.**
8. **Best-effort erasure.**

## Target design

One evolving committee per epoch for the whole application, as in Fig. 1.

- **Epochs.** An epoch has a fixed length in rounds. Epoch e runs a nominating window, which fixes committee C(e+1), then a handover window, after which C(e) erases.
- **Committee selection (§3.1.2).**
  - Each member runs VRF sortition once per epoch on the epoch seed; it is eligible with probability about C/N (stake-weighted later).
  - Every winner posts exactly one nomination: a fresh ephemeral key pair, with its secret sealed to a uniformly chosen member's per-epoch key (done today).
  - The holding committee is every valid nomination in the window, ordered by ephemeral public key. Slots and "lowest rank wins" go away.
- **What the committee holds: bucket keys.** Bucket b covers capsules expiring in [b·W, (b+1)·W). The committee holds a share of every live bucket key. A capsule is encrypted to its bucket's public key, so there is no per-capsule dealer and no per-capsule committee.
- **Bucket keys come from the committee.** They are generated by distributed key generation (DKG) carried in the handover message, so no dealer is trusted after genesis. Each dealer adds a fresh random contribution. The first t valid dealings define the key, so at least one of them is honest.
- **Handover (§3.1.3).** Each holder of C(e) posts one message ("speak once"), which contains:
  - for every live bucket, a re-sharing of its share to C(e+1), with public commitments;
  - dealings for new buckets;
  - decryption partials for authorized capsule requests.

  Before posting, the holder posts its next nomination key and erases everything else (step 4). It posts from its own account. The relayer is no longer needed, because posting reveals the holder only after it has erased.
- **Next committee.** Each member of C(e+1) checks all messages, takes the first t valid ones in key order, and combines its subshares with Lagrange coefficients. Every member sees the same chain data and validity is checked against public data, so all honest members pick the same set.
- **Expiry.** A bucket that has ended is not re-shared. After that handover, nobody holds it, under the paper's assumptions: fewer than t corrupt holders per epoch and secure erasure. A capsule expires with a granularity of its bucket width plus one epoch.
- **Release (§3.1.4, Def. 2.2).**
  - For an authorized request, each holder puts a decryption partial for the capsule in its handover message, sealed to the recipient's request key, with a proof.
  - The recipient checks the partials and combines the first t valid ones.
  - Release takes up to one epoch.
- **Binding against replay.** The sender proves knowledge of the capsule's encryption randomness, bound to the capsule's policy hash. Nobody can then register another capsule's ciphertext under their own policy to get it decrypted.

What changes for users: capsule secrets are no longer isolated from each other. An adversary that corrupts t members of one epoch's committee exposes every live bucket in that epoch. This is the paper's trade-off, and committee size is what controls it.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Options | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | What the committee holds | Per-capsule secrets re-shared each epoch (cost grows with the number of capsules); bucket keys (cost grows with the number of live buckets); one master key (no expiry) | **Bucket keys.** Packed secret sharing (several buckets in one polynomial) can cut the cost further. |
| D2 | Share encryption with verifiable re-sharing (§2.6) | Pairing-based publicly verifiable secret sharing (PVSS) on BLS12-381 with group-element shares; Groth's chunked-ElGamal non-interactive DKG; class-group PVSS; Paillier (the paper's DCR route) | **Pairing-based.** `@noble/curves` (already in the dependency tree) can implement it in TypeScript. It is compact and checked with pairing equations, and group-element shares are enough for a pairing-based key encapsulation (KEM). See the sketch below. |
| D3 | Where handover data lives | Boxes; app-call arguments in blocks with an on-chain digest per message; off-chain storage with on-chain hashes | **App-call arguments.** This is the paper's broadcast channel (§2.1). Boxes lock 400 µAlgo of deposit per byte. Off-chain storage adds a data-availability assumption the paper doesn't make. |
| D4 | Committee size and threshold | The paper's Chernoff bounds; exact tails | **Exact tails** (table below). Choose f and the failure probability per deployment, and derive t from the bound. |
| D5 | Sortition seed | Today's single-operator beacon; Algorand's block seed (`block BlkSeed` of a fixed past round); a threshold VRF run by the committee | **Block seed.** It fits the paper's resettable sortition (Fig. 2): proposers can re-draw only by withholding blocks. Keep the beacon as an option and consider a committee VRF later. |
| D6 | Membership and weighting (§1.2.2, §4) | Admin-admitted members (today); any online account, stake-weighted with `voter_params_get` and `online_stake` | **Stake-weighted and permissionless.** Members pay their own box deposits. Recovery after compromise means posting a new per-epoch key, which is already done. |
| D7 | Release timing | Inside the handover message (speak once); at any time through the relayer | **Speak once first.** A fast path can be added later as a documented deviation. |

## Feasibility numbers

Committee size C and threshold t, from exact Poisson tails of the §3.2 process with N ≫ C. The adversary may redraw the sortition 2^k1 times. The same failure bound applies to secrecy and to liveness, per epoch.

| Seed redraws | Failure per epoch | f = 1% | f = 5% | f = 10% | f = 20% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2^64 (paper) | 2^-128 | C 256, t 75 | C 489, t 197 | C 952, t 438 | C 5,246, t 2,630 |
| 2^8 | 2^-64 | 111, 29 | 210, 79 | 402, 176 | 2,189, 1,075 |
| none | 2^-64 | 107, 27 | 198, 72 | 378, 162 | 2,060, 1,003 |
| none | 2^-40 | 64, 16 | 120, 44 | 230, 99 | 1,243, 605 |

For comparison, the paper's Chernoff bounds give 889 for its 5% row; our implementation of them reproduces the paper's table to within 2%.

Handover data per held secret per epoch, with the sketch below, is 240·C² + 48·C·t bytes: 240 bytes per subshare plus 48 bytes per coefficient commitment. Phase 0 measured the cost on LocalNet. Each app call carries 20,460 bytes (16,384 of arguments plus a 4,096-byte note) for 2,741 µAlgo when the network is idle. That is 2.4 times cheaper per byte than calls within the base limits.

| C, t | Data per epoch | App calls | Fees at the LocalNet rate | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 120, 44 | 3.54 MiB | 240 | 0.66 Algo | measured |
| 200, 72 | 9.81 MiB | 600 | 1.65 Algo (at least two 5 MiB blocks) | measured |
| 490, 197 | 59.4 MiB | 3,430 | 9.4 Algo | computed |
| 889, 425 (paper, 5%) | 198 MiB | 10,668 | 29 Algo (at least 40 blocks) | computed |

The paper's 5% parameters cost too much per epoch on a shared chain. The analysis assumes N ≫ C, so anonymity needs a population in the thousands. LocalNet tests use small overridden parameters; they test the mechanics, not the security.

## Phase 0 results (done 2026-10-09)

**Gate: passed, for committees up to about 200 and a few live buckets per epoch.** Measured on an Apple M5 with Node v24.15.0 and LocalNet algod 5.0.1, single-threaded.

**Chosen parameters.** Use f = 5%, and assume the block seed gives the adversary up to 2^8 redraws, through withheld proposals.
- TestNet: failure 2^-40 per epoch gives **C = 130, t = 50**.
- Production-grade: 2^-64 gives **C = 210, t = 79**.
- LocalNet/CI: small overrides that test mechanics only.

`npm run params` in `crypto-spike/` prints the full table. Its test checks the paper's table to within 2%.

**Prototype.** `crypto-spike/src/pvss.ts` (unreviewed) implements the sketch. Its tests confirm the algebra:
- a committee-generated key survives two handovers;
- a tampered subshare, a wrong commitment, a missing proof of knowledge or a duplicate dealer drops only that dealing;
- shares from different epochs don't combine;
- verified decryption partials from t holders reproduce the sender's KEM key;
- a capsule's proof fails under another policy label.

**Cost per member per held secret per epoch** (`npm run bench`):

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 64 | 16 | 0.40 s | 5.4 s | 23.5 s | 0.16 s | 0.03 s | 0.02 s | 0.05 s | 15.8 KB | 0.98 MiB |
| 120 | 44 | 0.84 s | 15.8 s | 59.2 s | 0.48 s | 0.02 s | 0.02 s | 0.14 s | 30.2 KB | 3.54 MiB |
| 200 | 72 | 1.3 s | 39.6 s | 142.5 s | 0.97 s | 0.02 s | 0.02 s | 0.25 s | 50.3 KB | 9.81 MiB |

**Chain** (`npm run probe` in `policy-app/`, LocalNet):
- **Limits and fees.** One call carries up to 16,384 bytes of arguments and a 4,096-byte note; one byte more is rejected. The minimum fee covers 2,048 bytes of arguments and a 1,024-byte note. Beyond that, the idle network charged about 0.1 µAlgo per extra byte.
- **Posting an epoch.** C = 120 took 240 calls in 15 groups; C = 200 took 600 calls in 38 groups. Dev mode seals a block per group, so this does not test real block capacity.
- **Reading it back.** A reader rebuilt every seat's running digest from raw blocks through algod: 120/120 and 200/200 match the chain, read in 0.1–0.2 s.
- **Block seed.** `block BlkSeed` reads only rounds in (LastValid − 1002, FirstValid). With a 10-round validity window, that reaches about 990 rounds back.
- **Stake.** `voter_params_get` and `online_stake` return voting balances and total online stake on LocalNet.

**What this changes:**
1. **D3 holds**, using the extended limits. At C = 200, a held secret costs about 1.6 Algo of fees per epoch across the committee at idle rates. Phase 1 posts 20 KB chunks and keeps the per-seat on-chain digest the probe used.
2. **D5 holds with one constraint.** The contract must copy the epoch's block seed into state within about 990 rounds of the seed round, with a short validity window. Sortition checks then use the stored copy.
3. **Readers face a deadline.** Non-archival nodes keep only the last 1,000–1,321 blocks (Algorand docs and forum; not measured, since LocalNet is archival). Each member must read and verify a handover before it ages out, or read from an archival node.
4. **Phase 2 must speed up the cheating case.** Isolating one bad dealing costs 3.6 times an honest epoch, because every sub-batch repeats C pairings. Options:
   - reuse the Miller-loop precomputations for each epk_k;
   - spread verification across cores (10 here);
   - try shares in G1 with commitments in G2. This variant has fewer G2 operations and 192-byte subshares; that is an estimate, not a measurement.
5. **D6 holds.** The stake opcodes are usable.
6. **Budget.** At C = 200, each live bucket costs about 10 MiB, 1.6 Algo and roughly 45 s of one core per member per epoch. Seven live buckets would need multiple cores and epochs of about 30 minutes or more, or packed secret sharing.

**Not measured:** real-network block capacity and congestion fees; retention on TestNet; multi-core verification; the G1-share variant.

## Phase 1 and 2 results (done 2026-10-09)

**Both phases meet their "done when" lists**, on LocalNet and in-process. Everything is still unreviewed. Workers don't run epochs yet (Phase 3), and capsules don't use bucket keys yet (Phase 4).

**Phase 1** adds the `EpochCommittee` app (`policy-app/smart_contracts/epoch_committee/`) and `policy-app/src/epochs.ts`. Three LocalNet tests cover it:
- **Sortition.** The committee is exactly the VRF-eligible members (binomial around N·p). Each eligible member nominates once. Ineligible, late-enrolled, copied-proof, duplicate and out-of-window nominations are rejected.
- **Seats.** Seats are numbered in signing-key order, and each opens only for its nominee.
- **Seed.** The captured seed equals the block's seed.
- **Messages.** Readers rebuild each message from blocks and match the on-chain digest.
- **Cleanup.** Old committees are deleted after their epoch.

The handover test also runs a complete handover through the chain, between two real committees, using the Phase 2 library:
- the first committee holds an honest dealer's bucket;
- each holder claims its seat and posts one encoded message, which creates a second, committee-generated bucket;
- one holder posts 50 KB of garbage instead;
- the next committee drops it and reconstructs the original secret.

Phase 1 departs from the plan in four ways:
- **Seat records are keyed by (committee, nominator) on-chain.** The key order is computed off-chain. Seats whose keys repeat another seat's are all dropped, so a nominator can't front-run an honest seat by copying its keys; it can only spend its own nomination to cancel that seat.
- **No signature check at finalize.** Instead, a holder claims its seat once: the seat's one-time key signs the holder's account, and from then on only that account may post chunks. This is one signature check per seat instead of per message, and the holder reveals itself only when it posts, as in the paper.
- **Windows.** Nominations for the next committee take rounds 1…nominationRounds of each epoch. The current committee's handover takes the following handoverRounds rounds, all inside the epoch. Nominators seal seeds to the nominee's key for the nomination epoch.
- **Funding.** The admin still pre-funds every box (a seat box is 121,700 µAlgo). That changes with Phase 5.

**Phase 2** adds `crypto-spike/src/ecpss.ts` on top of `pvss.ts`, with 4 tests (35 in crypto-spike). They show:
- every live bucket survives 10 handovers through committees of 5–8 seats, each with up to t − 1 random cheaters (silent, lying to a seat, re-sharing another value, or forging a contribution proof);
- committee-generated keys never change their public key, and shares from different committees don't combine;
- an expired bucket is never re-dealt, so neither the chain nor the next committee ever holds it again;
- the capsule KEM opens from t verified partials and skips a bad one;
- a holder refuses a u whose proof belongs to another policy;
- the wire format round-trips and decoding is strict: lengths, curve and subgroup checks, identity points, entry kinds, duplicates.

Proofs of knowledge for new keys are bound to the committee, bucket and dealer.

**Cost of the cheating case: not improved.** Reusing each epoch key's pairing precomputation changed nothing measurable (37.2 s honest and 138.8 s with one cheater at C = 200, against 38.0 s and 139.2 s with it), so it was removed. The real cost is the two G2 multi-scalar multiplications over C² points per batch, which the bisection repeats. Spreading work across cores is the remaining lever and moves to Phase 3. Moving shares to G1 is estimated at about 10% and is not worth doing.

## Phase 3 results (done 2026-10-09)

**It meets the "done when" list on LocalNet.** The pieces are:
- `policy-app/src/epoch-worker.ts`;
- schedule parameters added to `EpochCommittee`: the threshold t, the bucket width and lead, and the genesis committee;
- `crypto-spike/src/parallel.ts`.

`test/epoch-workers.test.ts` drives 10 member workers in lockstep through 22 handovers, with four outages of one or two members for one or two epochs each. It has passed twice in a row and takes about 3 minutes. It shows that:
- every member, including the ones that were offline, processes every handover;
- no bucket is lost, and all members agree on each bucket's public key;
- a bucket's key and reconstructed secret never change while it lives;
- buckets appear and disappear exactly as scheduled;
- per the indexer, each seat's claim and finalize happen once, from the seat's own holder, after that member posted its key for the next epoch;
- every speaker is a member account, so there is no relayer, and nobody speaks while offline;
- old committees are deleted, and workers report no errors.

**Each epoch, a worker:**
1. posts its keys;
2. nominates if eligible;
3. opens its seats when nominations close, then erases that epoch's key;
4. as a holder, waits until its next-epoch key is posted, then builds its message, erases its shares, claims the seat and posts from its own account;
5. after each handover window, processes the handover: it decodes and verifies on the thread pool and combines shares for its seats in the next committee. It catches up in order after an outage.

**What the test found, now fixed:**
- **Stale seat records.** Processing reused the seat list from before anyone posted, so every message looked missing.
- **Old committees exhausted the app's balance.** A committee leaves about 1.2 Algo of boxes, and nominations failed after about 15 committees. Members now take turns deleting the committee three back. The trade-off: a member offline for more than about two epochs can no longer rebuild the messages it missed.
- **Silent failures.** The worker swallowed errors and never retried. It now reports errors as events and retries a failed nomination within its window.

**Cost: decoding dominates, which Phase 0 missed.**
- Every received point needs a subgroup check: about 0.63 s per dealing at C = 200, or about 126 s per bucket per epoch on one thread, against 37 s for the batched checks.
- Batching subgroup checks is not sound, because a small-order component vanishes under a random coefficient with probability 1/ℓ. Uncompressed points save only about 25% of the decode time and double the data.
- So `VerifyPool` decodes and checks groups of dealers on every core. On this Apple M5 (4 performance and 6 efficiency cores) decode plus verify takes 5.4 / 15.9 / 40.7 s at C = 64 / 120 / 200, about 4 times faster than one thread. The efficiency cores add little.
- Every batch repeats about C pairings, so one task per thread beats smaller tasks. Scaling on servers with uniform cores is not measured.
- Revised budget: at C = 200, each member spends about 40 s of a 10-core laptop per live bucket per epoch. Epochs therefore need to be several minutes long or more, depending on how many buckets are live.

**Departures:**
- Release partials are not in the message yet; that is Phase 4.
- An outage here means a member keeps its memory. A restarted member would have to replay every handover since genesis from an archival node, which is not implemented.
- Tests step the workers once per round; real workers poll.

## Crypto sketch for D2 (review before implementing)

This works in BLS12-381 with generators g1 ∈ G1 and g2 ∈ G2 and pairing e. The secret is the group element S = s·g2. The public key is P = s·g1.

- **Holder k.** Its share is S_k = σ_k·g2, with public commitment V_k = σ_k·g1. Its ephemeral key is a scalar x_k with epk_k = x_k·g2, derived from the nominator's seed as the one-time keys are today.
- **Re-share by holder j.**
  - Pick a_1…a_(t-1). The commitments are A_0 = V_j (already public) and A_l = a_l·g1.
  - For seat k, let M = S_j + (Σ_l a_l·k^l)·g2. Send the ElGamal ciphertext (R2 = r·g2, B = M + r·epk_k) together with R1 = r·g1.
  - Anyone can verify: e(R1, g2) = e(g1, R2) and e(g1, B) = e(Σ_l k^l·A_l, g2) · e(R1, epk_k). These checks can be batched across all subshares.
- **Combine.** Seat k sums S'_k = Σ λ_j·(B_j − x_k·R2_j) over the first t valid dealers. Its new commitment Σ λ_j·Σ_l k^l·A_(j,l) is public.
- **DKG.** The same message, but with A_0 = z·g1 for a fresh z and a Schnorr proof of knowledge of z (against rogue-key attacks).
- **Capsule KEM.** The sender picks r and sets U = r·g1 and K = e(P, g2)^r. It derives the payload key as HKDF(K, U, policy hash) and posts U with a Schnorr proof of r bound to the policy hash.
- **Release.**
  - Holder k computes the partial D_k = e(U, S_k).
  - It proves that the same S_k lies behind both e(g1, S_k) = e(V_k, g2) and D_k, using a sigma protocol for S ↦ (e(g1, S), e(U, S)).
  - It seals the partial and proof to the recipient's request key with HPKE.
  - The recipient computes K = Π D_k^(λ_k).

Precedents: pairing-based PVSS in SCRAPE (Cascudo and David, 2017), and group-element secret keys in aggregatable DKG (Gurkan et al., 2021). Plain ElGamal is not known to meet the paper's requirement of secrecy when some receivers' keys are later opened (RIND-SO, §2.6). The paper suggests CHK05's DDH-based receiver non-committing encryption instead. This remains open.

## Phases

**Phase 0: parameters and feasibility (gate). Done 2026-10-09; passed. See Phase 0 results.**
- Add the committee-size calculator (exact tails plus the paper's bounds) to the repo and choose the TestNet parameters.
- Benchmark the sketch in TypeScript at C = 64, 120 and 200: dealing, batch verification and combining.
- On LocalNet, measure handover bytes per app call and per group, block scanning through algod within its retained window, and fees per epoch.
- Gate: a parameter set whose handover fits the network's per-epoch budget and whose verification finishes inside the handover window. Otherwise, revisit D1 and D2 (packing, a lower f).

**Phase 1: epoch committees on-chain. Done 2026-10-09; see Phase 1 and 2 results.**
- Deploy a new app; the current one has no update path. It needs:
  - an epoch clock;
  - an epoch seed copied from `block BlkSeed` into state within about 990 rounds of the seed round (Phase 0);
  - one sortition per member per epoch;
  - seat records ordered by ephemeral key, with no slots.
- Add a message log. `postChunk` carries data in app arguments and keeps a running on-chain digest per seat. `finalize` checks the seat's one-time Ed25519 signature over that digest.
- Keep the per-epoch nomination keys, VRF verification and the fail-closed chain reader.
- Done when:
  - committee size varies around C;
  - seats are ordered by key;
  - a holder's chunks read back from blocks through algod match the on-chain digest;
  - old epochs' records can be deleted.

**Phase 2: ECPSS library, off-chain. Done 2026-10-09; see Phase 1 and 2 results.**
- In `crypto-spike/`, add:
  - §3.1 sharing, with mock committee selection;
  - handover with pairing-checked dealings, and reconstruction;
  - bucket DKG;
  - the threshold KEM with proven partials;
  - the sender's proof of knowledge.
- Done when:
  - a secret survives 10 handovers with random honest/corrupt mixes;
  - dealings with bad proofs are ignored;
  - shares from different epochs don't combine;
  - a dropped bucket can't be recovered from the next committee's state;
  - a replayed U under another policy is refused.

**Phase 3: workers run epochs on LocalNet. Done 2026-10-09; see Phase 3 results.**
- Each epoch, the worker:
  1. nominates if eligible;
  2. opens its seats after nominations close;
  3. at handover, builds its message (re-shares, new-bucket dealings, release partials);
  4. posts its next nomination key and erases that epoch's secrets;
  5. posts the message from its own account.
- Next-committee members verify and combine.
- Done when:
  - it runs for 20+ epochs with members going offline within the liveness margin;
  - the indexer shows exactly one handover message per holder per epoch, sent after its key rotation;
  - no relayer is involved.

**Phase 4: capsules on bucket keys. Size: medium.**
- Registration carries the policy, U, the proof, the payload hash and the bucket. Request binding stays as today. Releases come from handover messages. Update Capsule Lab.
- Done when:
  - private release works;
  - requests are refused before opening, after expiry, from the wrong wallet and for a replayed U;
  - a capsule's bucket is unrecoverable after its last handover.

**Phase 5: stake-weighted, permissionless membership. Size: medium.**
- Seats are proportional to online stake (Algorand-style binomial sortition, verified on-chain). Members fund their own boxes, and the admin gate goes away.
- Done when seat counts track stake over many epochs and accounts with no online stake can't nominate.

**Phase 6: hardening and TestNet. Size: medium.**
- **Erasure.** Keep key bytes in buffers we control instead of non-extractable WebCrypto keys, use one short-lived process per epoch's secrets, and disable swap and core dumps. Erasure is still not provable.
- **Liveness.** Track the count of valid messages against t, and refuse new capsules when the margin is thin. A failed handover loses every live bucket.
- **Infrastructure.** Give each member its own algod node, then run on TestNet with real fees and timings.

## Open questions for review

- **The sketch's assumptions.** Does S = s·g2 stay hidden given P = s·g1 and all the commitments? Which assumption does the KEM need? How much bias can selective aborts introduce into the DKG?
- **E2 under receiver selective opening.** The paper requires RIND-SO, which plain ElGamal doesn't have a proof for.
- **E1 anonymity.** HPKE over X25519 depends on the paper's unproven Conjecture 1.
- **Real seed redraws.** How many re-draws do block-seed proposers actually get? This sets k1.
- **Smaller committees.** Newer committee-size work might shrink C further: eprint 2026/919, "Towards Practical Committee Sizes in YOSO MPC", and 2026/1101, "Tail-Hammer". Only the titles have been seen so far; the papers haven't been read.

## Kept and removed

**Kept:**
- per-epoch nomination keys;
- VRF sortition with on-chain `vrf_verify`;
- HPKE for nominations;
- the fail-closed chain reader;
- the immutable app;
- Puya TS.

**Removed:**
- per-capsule slots and committees;
- per-capsule Shamir deposits, and the sender as dealer;
- the relayer for custody traffic;
- the dependence on a single-operator beacon.

`algorand-hidden-v1` stays until Phase 4 replaces it.

**Out of scope:** the paper's Appendix A variant (0.38 resilience against a slow adversary, with proofs linear in N) and the paper's MPC/obfuscation applications.
